# Demand Evidence Methodology

## Why public data

CarbonRadar SME v0.2 supports the Big Data Systems final project requirement for evidence of demand and willingness to pay without user interviews. The project uses curated public-data evidence so the workflow is reproducible, explainable, and reviewable offline.

The pipeline does not scrape websites, call live APIs, or use credentialed services. Source pages are manually curated into CSV seed datasets with source name, URL, access date, notes, and confidence level.

## Evidence categories

- Official regulatory sources show climate, carbon-fee, disclosure, and factor-publication pressure.
- Market size sources support the addressable SME and manufacturing context.
- Competitor pricing benchmarks show that carbon accounting, ESG reporting, and advisory alternatives exist; where pricing is not public, price fields remain empty and notes explain the limitation.
- ESG job posting examples show hiring signals for ESG, carbon inventory, ISO 14064, and carbon management roles.
- Public procurement examples show public-sector demand for greenhouse gas inventory, ESG reporting, sustainability reporting, and carbon reduction services.
- Willingness-to-pay assumptions are internal hypotheses for project planning and are not presented as verified market facts.

## Validation

`validate_evidence_sources()` checks that each dataset has its required columns and that every row has `source_name`, `source_url`, `date_accessed`, and a valid `confidence_level`.

Allowed confidence levels are:

- `high`
- `medium`
- `low`
- `needs_verification`

Validation returns a report of issues and never drops rows.

## Scoring logic

The deterministic scoring model totals 100 points:

- regulatory pressure: 25
- market size: 20
- willingness to pay: 20
- competitor benchmark: 15
- hiring or procurement signal: 20

Confidence weights are:

- high: 1.0
- medium: 0.75
- low: 0.5
- needs_verification: 0.25

Each sub-score uses:

```text
max_points * min(weighted_evidence_count / target_count, 1)
```

Targets are 5 regulatory rows, 4 market-size rows, 5 willingness-to-pay assumptions, 4 competitor rows, and 6 combined hiring/procurement rows.

Competitor rows without published prices still count when notes explain that pricing is quote-based or unavailable. Otherwise, missing-price rows count at half confidence.

## Interpretation

- `>=80`: Strong public-data support
- `>=60`: Moderate public-data support
- `<60`: Weak public-data support / needs more validation

These labels describe public-data support for the demand hypothesis. They do not prove verified customer demand or confirmed willingness to pay.

## Limitations

- Public webpages may change after the recorded `date_accessed`.
- Job postings and procurement examples are dynamic signals, not permanent facts.
- Quote-based competitor pricing limits benchmark precision.
- Willingness-to-pay rows are hypotheses, not confirmed buyer behavior.
- Public-data evidence does not replace interviews, pilots, or paid customer discovery.

## Mapping to the final project requirement

The report generated by `python -m carbonradar.cli build-demand-report` provides a reproducible artifact for "Evidence of Demand and Willingness to Pay." It shows demand pressure from regulation, SME market context, hiring/procurement signals, competitor presence, and explicit pricing hypotheses while clearly separating verified public evidence from assumptions.
